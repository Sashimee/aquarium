# Fish behaviour model

One line per behaviour: what the code does, where the idea or number comes from, and the
parameter that controls it. Parameters in `STEERING` live in `src/world.ts`; per-species values
come from the `behaviour` and `locomotion` blocks of `content/species/*.json`. Rows marked
**heuristic** are tuned by eye or by the tests rather than taken from a measurement. Treat them as
open to correction.

## Conventions

- **Tank frame:** the world's config picks it, and food, startles, decor, anchors and snapshots
  all use it. With `space` it is tank space from `@aquarium/shared` (metres, y = 0 under the
  bottom glass, the back pane at −Z, the viewer at +Z), the scene's own frame, and the floor is
  the substrate heightfield (`sampleFloor`). With the legacy `tank` it is a box centred on the
  substrate surface with a flat floor at y = 0.
- **Body frame:** +Z forward, +Y dorsal, +X the fish's left. Positive yaw turns left, positive
  pitch raises the nose, positive roll lowers the right flank (`quatFromYawPitchRoll`).
- **`depthBand`:** `[low, high]` as fractions of the water column measured from the substrate:
  0 is the sand under the fish and 1 is the surface, so on sloped sand the band follows the slope.
- **Body length (BL, L):** standard length, snout to caudal peduncle, as in `bodyLengthM`,
  `birthSizeM`, `maxSizeM` and every `*BodyLengths` parameter. The renderer scales each model so
  its standard length matches, and the caudal fin adds the rest of the total length. `maxSizeM`
  is FishBase's adult maximum, converted from TL with its length-length relationship where it
  only gives TL; `src/sizes.test.ts` holds the sources.
- **Step:** fixed 30 Hz (`STEP_HZ`). All randomness comes from the seeded sfc32 `Rng`, so the
  same seed and inputs reproduce the same trajectory bit for bit on one JS engine.
- **Snapshot:** `Float32Array`, `SNAPSHOT_STRIDE = 17` floats per fish: position xyz,
  quaternion xyzw, speed (m/s), yaw rate (rad/s, for tail-beat and bend), state (`FishState`),
  thrust (0–1 fraction of maximum caudal thrust; 0 while coasting), the walker fields leg
  phase, tail-flip progress and attached (all 0 for swimmers; see Walkers), then display and
  gill flare (0–1; see Toys), then pectoral fan (0–1; see Brood).
- **Species parameters:** `BEHAVIOUR` in `src/behaviour.ts` holds every species-behaviour
  tunable. Behaviours the shared schema has no field for are switched on by
  `behaviour.traits` (`TRAITS` in `src/species.ts`): `shoal`, `air-gulp`, `glass-grazer`,
  `rock-grazer`, `anemone-host`, `cave-dweller`, `flare-display`. An unknown or conflicting
  trait fails species loading. `gait: hover` gives a fish a self-chosen station.
- **Traits and gaits:** `Behaviour.planDefault` tries, in order, the forage gait, glass grazing,
  a home (anchor or station) and free swimming, so species loading **rejects** a trait that this
  order never reaches: `air-gulp` needs `gait: forage`; `anemone-host` needs a gait other than
  `forage` and no `glass-grazer`; `rock-grazer` needs a gait other than `hover` or `forage` and no
  `anemone-host` or `cave-dweller`. `glass-grazer` works on every gait: a forager with it forages
  while it is hungry and food it eats lies settled, and grazes glass otherwise (Forager glass
  grazing). Such a forager with `air-gulp` loads, but gulps only while it forages, never from the
  glass; no species ships with that combination. An
  anemone host that forages or grazes glass is rejected rather than half-run: the anchor would
  still set its flee target, feeding leash, territory centre and mate check while the fish never
  stays at it. A `cave-dweller` works on every gait: sheltering comes before that order, so a
  forager or a glass grazer with a cave keeps its own gait by day and retreats to the cave at rest
  and after a startle. Such substrate fish shelter on the cave floor, in their forage band, rather
  than at the mouth's mid-height, which would pin them against the floor and the cave wall.
- **Per-trait parameters:** a species can override some of those defaults with optional
  `behaviour` objects, read by `toSimSpecies`. A field left out keeps the `BEHAVIOUR` default.
  `airGulp.perHour` → `gulpsPerHour` (needs `air-gulp`); `grazing.meanIntervalS`/`minS`/`maxS` →
  `grazeMeanIntervalS`/`grazeMinS`/`grazeMaxS` (needs `rock-grazer`); `flare.minS`/`maxS` →
  `flareMinS`/`flareMaxS` (needs `flare-display`); `station.radiusBodyLengths`/`restationMinS`/
  `restationMaxS` → `stationRadiusBodyLengths`/`restationMinS`/`restationMaxS` (needs a home:
  `gait: hover`, `anemone-host` or `cave-dweller`, because an anemone or cave fish placed without
  its anchor holds a self-chosen station instead, and also a gait other than `forage` and no
  `glass-grazer`, since those come before a home); `refuge.afterStartleS` →
  `shelterAfterStartleS` (needs `cave-dweller`); `excursion.meanIntervalS`/`leashBodyLengths` →
  `excursionMeanIntervalS`/`hostLeashBodyLengths` (needs `anemone-host`). Species loading
  **rejects** an object the sim would never read, because it would otherwise be ignored silently
  and a warning in a worker is easy to miss. It also rejects a min/max pair that is out of order
  after the merge, such as `grazing: { minS: 50 }` against the default `grazeMaxS` of 4.
- **Feeding:** every feeding tunable is in `FEEDING` (`src/feeding.ts`). Food arrives each tick
  as `[id, x, y, z, typeIndex]` float32 records (`FOOD_STRIDE = 5`), `typeIndex` in the order of
  the shared `foodTypeSchema` (`FOOD_TYPES`). The sim never moves food; it reports
  `ate {fishId, foodId, portion}` and gameplay removes what was eaten. Portions are the lifecycle
  model's feed portions; `FOOD_PARTICLES` gives what one particle of each type holds. A species'
  `lifecycle.diet` lists foods most-preferred first.

### Circling bound

Tangs boxed in by rock and glass sometimes turn through a full 360° within 4 s: a turn out of a
dead end, or a lap round a rock. The tests measure each loop's radius as half the smallest span of
its path and allow none tighter than 0.35 body lengths and fewer than 2 loops per 10 fish-minutes.
Measured over seeds 1–8: the tightest yellow tang loop is 0.39 L at 0.33 loops per 10
fish-minutes, and the tightest blue tang loop is 0.40 L at 0.46 (before the turning-radius cap
they were 0.20 L and 0.17 L).

### Tang swimming mode

Both tangs carry `swimmingMode: labriform`: surgeonfishes (Acanthuridae) cruise on flapping
pectoral fins and keep the body and tail for bursts (Walker & Westneat 1997, J Exp Biol 200:1549).
PLAN §2.3 lists tangs under the carangiform spine wave, so the renderer should check which one it
uses.

## Curiosity and feeding-spot memory

A fish following a lure (a laser dot or fingertip on the glass, `World.setLure`) stays in
`FishState.Cruise`, and one waiting at its feeding spot in `FishState.Station`. Only fish with
nothing better to do take part: not feeding, chasing, handling food or holding territory, and
not in any state that already stops feeding. Constants are in `CURIOSITY`
(`packages/sim/src/curiosity.ts`); the species field is `behaviour.curiosity`. The sim keeps no
clock time, so the learnt interval between meals stands in for the time of day.

Spot memory is the same for every species: `spotLearnRate`, `spotMemoryTauS`, `mealGapS` and the
anticipation window are global constants with no species field, as the time-place studies cited
below give no per-species rates to tell them apart. Fish differ only in how hungry each is, which
scales the pull, and in the anchor leash.

Determinism: the RNG is drawn only while a lure is shown, so a world that never shows one draws
the same random numbers as it did before lures existed. Its trajectories can still differ from
before: once a tank has had two meals, fish wait at the learnt spot as the next comes due, so a
run with repeated feeding moves differently from one recorded before feeding-spot memory was
added. With at most one meal there is no learnt interval and nothing to wait for. The same seed
and inputs always give the same trajectory.

## Toys

`World.setToys` takes every toy in the tank: mirrors (centre of the glass, a horizontal normal
into the tank, 12 × 16 cm) and floating balls (centre and drift; 40 mm, 2.7 g, a table-tennis
ball). Resend a drifting ball as it moves. A toy with an `id` stays the same toy wherever it sits in
the list; one without is matched by its order among the id-less toys of its kind.
`World.takeToyNudges` (worker event `toyNudge`) reports each bump as a velocity change for the
ball, naming it by `toyId` (null for a ball without an id) and its index in the list, which gameplay applies. Only fish with nothing better to do take part, as for a lure; a fish
following a lure takes up no toy. A fish displaying at a mirror is in `FishState.Display`. The RNG
is drawn only while a toy is in view of a fish that would respond to it, so a tank without toys,
or with a mirror and no displaying species, runs exactly as before. Constants are in `TOYS`
(`packages/sim/src/toys.ts`).

## Brood

`World.setBrood` takes every clutch being tended (id, parent fish ids, a point within 2 mm of
rock, sand or glass) and every pregnant livebearer (fish id and term, 0 at conception to 1 at the
drop; a fish spawned `male` is refused); resend it whenever either changes, and resend a clutch
whose rock moved. A hiding spot that moved decor now covers is chosen again. `World.nestSite(parents)` says where a pair would lay, so the caller
can place the clutch, and draws no RNG. The drop is the caller's: it ends the pregnancy and adds
the fry with `addFish({ stage: 'fry' })` at the mother. `World.setStage` moves a growing fish on
to juvenile and adult without respawning it. Fish carry `stage` and `sex` from `FishSpawn`. A
parent at its clutch is in `FishState.Tend`; a hiding fry or near-term mother at its spot is in
`FishState.Shelter`. The RNG is drawn only for parents of a clutch, fry and near-term mothers, so
a tank with none runs exactly as before. Constants are in `BROOD` (`packages/sim/src/brood.ts`).

## Personality and coming to the glass

Each fish carries its own `boldness`, `greed` and `sociability`, each 0 to 1, rolled and stored by
the API with the fish (`personalitySchema` in `@aquarium/shared`) and given to the sim with the
spawn (`FishSpawn.personality`) or later (`World.setPersonality`, worker command `personality`).
A trait is used as its distance from 0.5, so a neutral fish (all 0.5, also what a fish without
one gets) has every factor exactly 1 and moves bit for bit as it did before personalities. Gains
are in `PERSONALITY` (`src/personality.ts`); the trait axes come from the literature, the gains
are **heuristic**. `World.setVisitor` (worker command `visitor`) tells the sim the owner is at the
front glass, with the tank response's `recentVisitDays` (0 to `VISIT_WINDOW_DAYS`), from which
the sim derives a familiarity 0 to 1 (`familiarityFromVisits`). The RNG is drawn only on the step a
familiar visitor arrives, so a tank nobody visits, or a first visit, runs as before.
Constants for the visit are in `GREETING` (`src/greeting.ts`).

## Walkers (shrimp, snails, hermit crabs)

A species with `locomotion.walking` walks on surfaces instead of swimming (`src/walk.ts`, `WALK`;
bout lengths in `BEHAVIOUR`). It stands 0.15 L off the surface under it, turns in that surface's
tangent plane and carries its heading over edges and into corners. States: `Walk` (13), `Pick`
(14), `TailFlip` (16), `Fall` (17), plus the shared `Shelter`, `Rest` and `Feed`; `Dance` (15) is
reserved for a walking cleaner (not modelled yet; the cleaner wrasse's station is under Cleaning station). Walkers do not school, hold station or
inspect lures. A shrimp (`swimmingMode: pleopod`) escapes with a tail-flip; a snail or hermit crab
(`swimmingMode: none`) never leaves the surface and withdraws into its shell instead.

**Speed limits:** `walkSpeedMps ≤ cruiseSpeedMps ≤ maxSpeedMps`, and a tail-flip's
`speedMps` is the mean of a half-sine pulse whose peak, π/2 × mean (`FLIP_PEAK_OVER_MEAN`), must
not exceed `maxSpeedMps`. Species loading throws otherwise. While walking, `cruiseSpeed` is the
walk speed.

## Clean-up crew

`src/crew.ts`, constants in `CREW`. The crew is whatever grazes or scavenges: walkers with the
`biofilm-grazer` trait pick film off any surface they stand on (`surface`), `glass-grazer` fish
rasp glass and decor (`glass`), tangs peck rock turf (`rock`), and walkers and bottom foragers
scavenge settled food. There are no crew-only species fields.

Two views of the same model, so a live tank and the lifecycle agree:

- **Live** (`World.setAlgae`, `World.takeCrewTally`, bridge `algae` command and `crew` event): each
  step a fish that is grazing (an attached grazer walker picking or walking with no food in view, a
  glass or rock grazer in `Graze`) clears `grazeAreaRate(L, speed)` × dt × cover m² of film; bites
  on settled food are summed in feed portions. Tallies are kept by fish id, so a fish removed when
  it dies keeps what it did before. Algae cover changes nothing about movement.
- **Offline** (`crewCleanup`, for the lifecycle's 10-minute step): each member's mean rate over its
  bout cycle (`algaeAreaPerHour`, `scavengeSweepPerHour`). Cover falls as dA/dt = −A·Σrate /
  grazable area (glass below the waterline plus the substrate, `cleanupSurfaces`), so it never goes
  below zero; settled food is found as dF/dt = −F·Σsweep / floor area and eaten up to each
  scavenger's room once its gut has emptied over the step; a full one leaves its share to hungrier
  ones. A member whose diet has none of the food types given in `uneatenFoodTypes` (every type when
  omitted, since the lifecycle keeps one total) does not look for it. A hungry forager that grazes
  glass sweeps for food it eats until less than the smallest particle (0.02 portions) is left, a
  time solved from the same decay, and grazes for the rest of the step; a sated one grazes all of
  it. Dead members do nothing. The live rate matches the offline one within a factor of two.

## Wave maker current

A reef or mangrove tank's wave maker drives a current (`src/waveMaker.ts`, `WAVE_MAKER`). The free
stream is a pure function of position and sim time (`FlowField`), so the scene can sample the same
water for plant and coral sway. With no wave maker, or one at 0 m/s, the world runs bit-identically
to a tank without one.

the decor field (walls without the water surface), so it never enters hard decor; it is not

## Not yet modelled

_Aulonocara_ territoriality is male-only in nature (Konings 2007; Seriously Fish), but `territorial` is one species flag with no sex, so a female _A. baenschi_ also holds a territory. A per-sex flag needs a field in the shared species schema.

Archerfish do not spit at prey above the water: there are no overhanging insects. Mudskippers
haul out only onto a bank anchor, not onto open mud. Bumblebee gobies do not breed, since there
is no cave anchor in the mangrove preset for the male to guard. A figure-8 puffer does not hunt
live snails.

Clownfish tend at the same rate through development, though both parents tend more as the eggs
mature (Green & McCormick 2005; Pacaro et al. 2023), and the night-only vigorous fanning, vigorous
mouthing and anemone biting around hatching (Pacaro et al. 2023) are not modelled. Pregnancy slows
cruising but not the fast start. Male livebearers do not court or chase pregnant females.

Mirror habituation lasts minutes, not the days over which Clayton & Hinde (1968) saw it wane
and recover, and a mirror does not answer back as Everett et al. (2025) found a rival must for
display to persist. The ball is not a collider: only the bump links a fish to it.

Snails and hermit crabs exist only as sim fixtures: there is no species content or rig for them
yet. A hermit crab does not change shells. Algae is one cover value, so grazers do not leave
visibly clean trails, and the crew does not prefer the dirtiest surfaces.

Area-restricted search after a find, learning which food is good, and food type affecting the
strike (suction versus biting) are not modelled. Feeding-spot memory knows one spot per fish
and no time of day, with rates shared by every species. Behaviour constants outside the per-trait
parameter objects are shared by every species.

Cleaning does not lower parasite load or stress in the lifecycle: there is no parasite field in
the contract to act on. Clients do not jolt at a bite and the wrasse does not punish or cheat
(Bshary & Grutter 2002), visits are not longer for larger or more parasitised clients, and the
wrasse does not dance to advertise its station or leave it to clean a client elsewhere.
