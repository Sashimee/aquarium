# Fish behaviour model

One line per behaviour: what the code does, where the idea or number comes from, and the
parameter that controls it. Parameters in `STEERING` live in `src/world.ts`; per-species values
come from the `behaviour` and `locomotion` blocks of `content/species/*.json`. Rows marked
**heuristic** are tuned by eye or by the tests rather than taken from a measurement. Treat them as
open to correction.

## Conventions

- **Tank frame:** the world's config picks it, and food, startles, decor, anchors and snapshots
  all use it. With `space` it is tank space from `@aquarium/shared` (metres, y = 0 under the
  bottom glass, the back pane at −Z, the viewer at +Z), the scene's own frame, and the floor is
  the substrate heightfield (`sampleFloor`). With the legacy `tank` it is a box centred on the
  substrate surface with a flat floor at y = 0.
- **Body frame:** +Z forward, +Y dorsal, +X the fish's left. Positive yaw turns left, positive
  pitch raises the nose, positive roll lowers the right flank (`quatFromYawPitchRoll`).
- **`depthBand`:** `[low, high]` as fractions of the water column measured from the substrate:
  0 is the sand under the fish and 1 is the surface, so on sloped sand the band follows the slope.
- **Body length (BL, L):** standard length, snout to caudal peduncle, as in `bodyLengthM`,
  `birthSizeM`, `maxSizeM` and every `*BodyLengths` parameter. The renderer scales each model so
  its standard length matches, and the caudal fin adds the rest of the total length. `maxSizeM`
  is FishBase's adult maximum, converted from TL with its length-length relationship where it
  only gives TL; `src/sizes.test.ts` holds the sources.
- **Step:** fixed 30 Hz (`STEP_HZ`). All randomness comes from the seeded sfc32 `Rng`, so the
  same seed and inputs reproduce the same trajectory bit for bit on one JS engine.
- **Snapshot:** `Float32Array`, `SNAPSHOT_STRIDE = 14` floats per fish: position xyz,
  quaternion xyzw, speed (m/s), yaw rate (rad/s, for tail-beat and bend), state (`FishState`),
  thrust (0–1 fraction of maximum caudal thrust; 0 while coasting), then the walker fields leg
  phase, tail-flip progress and attached (all 0 for swimmers; see Walkers).
- **Species parameters:** `BEHAVIOUR` in `src/behaviour.ts` holds every species-behaviour
  tunable. Behaviours the shared schema has no field for are switched on by
  `behaviour.traits` (`TRAITS` in `src/species.ts`): `shoal`, `air-gulp`, `glass-grazer`,
  `rock-grazer`, `anemone-host`, `cave-dweller`, `flare-display`. An unknown or conflicting
  trait fails species loading. `gait: hover` gives a fish a self-chosen station.
- **Traits and gaits:** `Behaviour.planDefault` tries, in order, the forage gait, glass grazing,
  a home (anchor or station) and free swimming, so species loading **rejects** a trait that this
  order never reaches: `air-gulp` needs `gait: forage`; `glass-grazer` needs a gait other than
  `forage`; `anemone-host` needs a gait other than `forage` and no `glass-grazer`; `rock-grazer`
  needs a gait other than `hover` or `forage` and no `anemone-host` or `cave-dweller`. An
  anemone host that forages or grazes glass is rejected rather than half-run: the anchor would
  still set its flee target, feeding leash, territory centre and mate check while the fish never
  stays at it. A `cave-dweller` works on every gait: sheltering comes before that order, so a
  forager or a glass grazer with a cave keeps its own gait by day and retreats to the cave at rest
  and after a startle. Such substrate fish shelter on the cave floor, in their forage band, rather
  than at the mouth's mid-height, which would pin them against the floor and the cave wall.
- **Per-trait parameters:** a species can override some of those defaults with optional
  `behaviour` objects, read by `toSimSpecies`. A field left out keeps the `BEHAVIOUR` default.
  `airGulp.perHour` → `gulpsPerHour` (needs `air-gulp`); `grazing.meanIntervalS`/`minS`/`maxS` →
  `grazeMeanIntervalS`/`grazeMinS`/`grazeMaxS` (needs `rock-grazer`); `flare.minS`/`maxS` →
  `flareMinS`/`flareMaxS` (needs `flare-display`); `station.radiusBodyLengths`/`restationMinS`/
  `restationMaxS` → `stationRadiusBodyLengths`/`restationMinS`/`restationMaxS` (needs a home:
  `gait: hover`, `anemone-host` or `cave-dweller`, because an anemone or cave fish placed without
  its anchor holds a self-chosen station instead, and also a gait other than `forage` and no
  `glass-grazer`, since those come before a home); `refuge.afterStartleS` →
  `shelterAfterStartleS` (needs `cave-dweller`); `excursion.meanIntervalS`/`leashBodyLengths` →
  `excursionMeanIntervalS`/`hostLeashBodyLengths` (needs `anemone-host`). Species loading
  **rejects** an object the sim would never read, because it would otherwise be ignored silently
  and a warning in a worker is easy to miss. It also rejects a min/max pair that is out of order
  after the merge, such as `grazing: { minS: 50 }` against the default `grazeMaxS` of 4.
- **Feeding:** every feeding tunable is in `FEEDING` (`src/feeding.ts`). Food arrives each tick
  as `[id, x, y, z, typeIndex]` float32 records (`FOOD_STRIDE = 5`), `typeIndex` in the order of
  the shared `foodTypeSchema` (`FOOD_TYPES`). The sim never moves food; it reports
  `ate {fishId, foodId, portion}` and gameplay removes what was eaten. Portions are the lifecycle
  model's feed portions; `FOOD_PARTICLES` gives what one particle of each type holds. A species'
  `lifecycle.diet` lists foods most-preferred first.

### Circling bound

Tangs boxed in by rock and glass sometimes turn through a full 360° within 4 s: a turn out of a
dead end, or a lap round a rock. The tests measure each loop's radius as half the smallest span of
its path and allow none tighter than 0.35 body lengths and fewer than 2 loops per 10 fish-minutes.
Measured over seeds 1–8: the tightest yellow tang loop is 0.39 L at 0.33 loops per 10
fish-minutes, and the tightest blue tang loop is 0.40 L at 0.46 (before the turning-radius cap
they were 0.20 L and 0.17 L).

### Tang swimming mode

Both tangs carry `swimmingMode: labriform`: surgeonfishes (Acanthuridae) cruise on flapping
pectoral fins and keep the body and tail for bursts (Walker & Westneat 1997, J Exp Biol 200:1549).
PLAN §2.3 lists tangs under the carangiform spine wave, so the renderer should check which one it
uses.

## Curiosity and feeding-spot memory

A fish following a lure (a laser dot or fingertip on the glass, `World.setLure`) stays in
`FishState.Cruise`, and one waiting at its feeding spot in `FishState.Station`. Only fish with
nothing better to do take part: not feeding, chasing, handling food or holding territory, and
not in any state that already stops feeding. Constants are in `CURIOSITY`
(`packages/sim/src/curiosity.ts`); the species field is `behaviour.curiosity`. The sim keeps no
clock time, so the learnt interval between meals stands in for the time of day.

Spot memory is the same for every species: `spotLearnRate`, `spotMemoryTauS`, `mealGapS` and the
anticipation window are global constants with no species field, as the time-place studies cited
below give no per-species rates to tell them apart. Fish differ only in how hungry each is, which
scales the pull, and in the anchor leash.

Determinism: the RNG is drawn only while a lure is shown, so a world that never shows one draws
the same random numbers as it did before lures existed. Its trajectories can still differ from
before: once a tank has had two meals, fish wait at the learnt spot as the next comes due, so a
run with repeated feeding moves differently from one recorded before feeding-spot memory was
added. With at most one meal there is no learnt interval and nothing to wait for. The same seed
and inputs always give the same trajectory.

## Walkers (shrimp)

A species with `locomotion.walking` walks on surfaces instead of swimming (`src/walk.ts`, `WALK`;
bout lengths in `BEHAVIOUR`). It stands 0.15 L off the surface under it, turns in that surface's
tangent plane and carries its heading over edges and into corners. States: `Walk` (13), `Pick`
(14), `TailFlip` (16), `Fall` (17), plus the shared `Shelter`, `Rest` and `Feed`; `Dance` (15) is
reserved for the cleaner station (not modelled yet). Walkers do not school, hold station or
inspect lures.

**Speed limits:** `walkSpeedMps ≤ cruiseSpeedMps ≤ maxSpeedMps`, and the tail-flip's
`speedMps` is the mean of a half-sine pulse whose peak, π/2 × mean (`FLIP_PEAK_OVER_MEAN`), must
not exceed `maxSpeedMps`. Species loading throws otherwise. While walking, `cruiseSpeed` is the
walk speed.
