# Fish behaviour model

One line per behaviour: what the code does, where the idea or number comes from, and the
parameter that controls it. Parameters in `STEERING` live in `src/world.ts`; per-species values
come from the `behaviour` and `locomotion` blocks of `content/species/*.json`. Rows marked
**heuristic** are tuned by eye or by the tests rather than taken from a measurement. Treat them as
open to correction.

## Conventions

- **Tank frame:** metres, origin at the centre of the substrate surface, +Y up to the water
  surface, X across the front glass, Z front to back.
- **Body frame:** +Z forward, +Y dorsal, +X the fish's left. Positive yaw turns left, positive
  pitch raises the nose, positive roll lowers the right flank (`quatFromYawPitchRoll`).
- **`depthBand`:** `[low, high]` as fractions of the water column measured from the substrate:
  0 is the floor and 1 is the surface.
- **Step:** fixed 30 Hz (`STEP_HZ`). All randomness comes from the seeded sfc32 `Rng`, so the
  same seed and inputs reproduce the same trajectory bit for bit on one JS engine.
- **Snapshot:** `Float32Array`, `SNAPSHOT_STRIDE = 10` floats per fish: position xyz,
  quaternion xyzw, speed (m/s), yaw rate (rad/s, for tail-beat and bend), state (`FishState`).

## Behaviours

| Behaviour                               | What the code does                                                                                                                                                                                             | Source                                                                                                                               | Parameter                                                                                                                |
| --------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Steering architecture                   | Each fish sums desired-velocity terms; an imminent wall scales the other terms down (prioritised blending).                                                                                                    | Reynolds 1987, "Flocks, herds and schools", SIGGRAPH 21(4):25; Reynolds 1999, "Steering behaviors for autonomous characters", GDC.   | `avoidPriority` 0.5 (**heuristic**)                                                                                      |
| Topological neighbours                  | Alignment and cohesion use the k nearest conspecifics, not every fish inside a radius.                                                                                                                         | Ballerini et al. 2008, PNAS 105:1232 (k ≈ 6–7 in starling flocks); topological interaction also fits fish schools in later work.     | `behaviour.schooling.topologicalNeighbours` (tetra 7, cory 5); capped by `perceptionRangeM`                              |
| Zones: repulsion, alignment, attraction | Separation inside ~1 body length, alignment to neighbours' velocity, attraction to their centroid.                                                                                                             | Couzin et al. 2002, J Theor Biol 218:1 (zone model).                                                                                 | `separationZoneBodyLengths` 1, `separationWeight` 2, `alignmentWeight` 1.5, `cohesionRatePerS` 1                         |
| School spacing                          | The weights above are calibrated so the mean nearest-neighbour distance in a tetra school sits near 1 BL (tested band 0.5–2 BL).                                                                               | Pitcher & Partridge 1979, Mar Biol 54:383 (NND ≈ 1 BL in cruising schools).                                                          | test `holds tetra shoals within literature bands`                                                                        |
| Speed regulation in schools             | Alignment averages neighbours' velocities, not just headings, so fish behind speed up and fish ahead slow down.                                                                                                | Katz et al. 2011, PNAS 108:18720 (speed changes dominate interactions in golden shiners).                                            | `alignmentWeight`                                                                                                        |
| Separation across species               | Repulsion from the 6 nearest fish of any species, zone scaled by both body lengths.                                                                                                                            | Couzin et al. 2002 repulsion zone; mixed-species sizing is **heuristic**.                                                            | `anyNeighbours` 6, `separationZoneBodyLengths`                                                                           |
| Spontaneous wandering                   | Turning rate follows an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process (persistent turning walker), and pitch has a slower OU drift.                                                                                               | Gautrais et al. 2009, J Math Biol 58:429 (persistent turning walker fitted to _Kuhlia mugil_).                                       | `wanderTurnSdRadPerS` 0.5, `wanderTurnTauS` 0.8, `wanderPitchSdRad` 0.12, `wanderPitchTauS` 2 (**heuristic** magnitudes) |
| Preferred depth band                    | Vertical desired velocity pulls the fish back into its band (kept off the floor and surface), with a capped climb rate.                                                                                        | Species zonation from husbandry literature (data); the restoring law is **heuristic**.                                               | `behaviour.depthBand`, `depthTauS` 2, `depthMaxSpeedFraction` 0.5                                                        |
| Speed scales with length                | Cruise and max speed are given per species and scale linearly with an individual's length (body lengths per second).                                                                                           | Bainbridge 1958, J Exp Biol 35:109 (speed ∝ length at a given tail-beat frequency).                                                  | `locomotion.cruiseSpeedMps`, `maxSpeedMps`, `FishSpawn.bodyLengthM`                                                      |
| Mass, thrust and drag                   | Mass scales with L³; quadratic drag ½ρC·L²·v²; thrust capped at the drag at max speed, so terminal speed equals max speed. Drag is integrated implicitly.                                                      | Lighthill 1971, Proc R Soc B 179:125; Anderson, McGillis & Grosenbaugh 2001, J Exp Biol 204:81 (drag coefficients of swimming fish). | `DRAG_COEFFICIENT_L2` 0.02 (order of magnitude from those data), `locomotion.massKg`, `speedResponseS` 0.4               |
| Turn-rate and pitch limits              | Yaw and pitch follow a P-controller clamped to the species' turn rate; pitch is clamped to the species' limit.                                                                                                 | Species data; cruising (not C-start) turn rates. Controller gain and smoothing are **heuristic**.                                    | `locomotion.maxTurnRateDegPerS`, `maxPitchDeg`, `turnGainPerS` 6, `turnSmoothingS` 0.1                                   |
| Reaction lag                            | The summed desired velocity is low-pass filtered so discrete neighbour-set changes do not twitch the heading.                                                                                                  | Fish visual response latencies are of order 0.1–0.2 s (startle responses are faster); value is **heuristic**.                        | `intentSmoothingS` 0.15                                                                                                  |
| Banking                                 | Roll leans into the turn in proportion to tanh of the centripetal acceleration v·ω, smoothed.                                                                                                                  | **Heuristic** visual cue; fish do roll into turns, but there is no clean scaling law used here.                                      | `maxBankRad` 35°, `bankReferenceAccel` 1 m/s², `bankSmoothingS` 0.2                                                      |
| Wall avoidance (containment)            | A probe ahead (½ L + v·lookahead + turning radius) samples the SDF; a proximity term catches walls beside or behind. The inward component is cancelled and the fish slides along the pane on a committed side. | Reynolds 1999 containment; committed side and slide are **heuristic** fixes for head-on dithering.                                   | `avoidLookaheadS` 0.8, `avoidMarginBodyLengths` 0.75, `proximityBodyLengths` 0.25, `avoidGradientBodyLengths` 0.5        |
| Hard collision constraint               | After integration, a fish closer than its radius to any surface is projected out along the SDF gradient (or reset), and speed into the wall is removed. Counted in `World.contacts`.                           | Standard position-based constraint; not a behaviour.                                                                                 | `collisionRadiusBodyLengths` 0.5                                                                                         |

## Not yet modelled (T10 and later)

Burst-and-coast gait, labriform hovering, bottom foraging and air-gulp darts, glass and decor
grazing, territoriality, anemone hosting, cave sheltering, C-start startle and its spread through
the school, night rest, feeding perception and competition, decor colliders with an incremental
SDF grid, and soft plant contact.
